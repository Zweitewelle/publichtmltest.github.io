Rosalind Franklin - A British Chemist born in 1920 and is renowned for her work on DNA. She famously studied coal, viruses, and eventually DNA, and would produce “Image 51” which would prove the double-helix structure. Although she had many discoveries and contributions, publishing many research paper during her lifetime, she was unfortunately overshadowed by her male colleagues. But many people after her death agreed she should have been awarded the Nobel Prize and her work started to be recognized more formally.

Srinivasa Ramanujan - Born in 1887, he was an Indian mathematician that was regarded as the greatest mathematicians of the time despite having no formal training. After developing a deep interest in math at a young age, at 16, he found George Shoobridge Carr’s book of thousands of theorems which he verified and expanded on. Despite being self taught he made huge contributions to the infinite series, number theory, and partition function. Although he died young, at 32 yrs, many loose leaf papers and unorganized notebooks would continue to fascinate, puzzle, and be examined by scholars for years to come. To this day, mathematicians still are gaining insight from his work that may even explain the phenomena of black holes!

Marie Curie - She was a Polish-French physicist born in 1867. Although she accomplished many feats in her life from being the first women professor in the University of Paris, to launching the Curie Family legacy, the most famous of them is perhaps her research of radiation which led to the discovery of polonium and radium. Due to her lifelong exposure and work of radiation, all her research papers, her coffin, and even cookbooks had to be later sealed with lead and everyone who comes in contact must wear protective clothing.

Li Shizhen - An influential Chinese scholar born in 1518 who compiled the Compendium of Materia Medica. It took 27 years to make it and his goal was to create a logical classification of medicinal herbs and how they could be used. Although he died before the official publishing of the book and that it was disregarded by the emperor at the time, it would go on to be extremely influential and Li Shizhen would come to be known as the father of modern medicine.

Nicolaus Copernicus - Born in 1473, he was a Polish astronomer and mathematician. Nowadays, we know the earth isn’t the center of the universe, but that wasn’t the case until Nicolaus came around. Rather than being praised for his breakthrough,though, he was met with controversy and his papers were even outright banned! It wasn’t until the year of his death, 1543, that his book of research was allowed to be published in Germany for the world to read.
